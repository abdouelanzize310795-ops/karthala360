How these figures are estimated
  1. Public traces: 25 confirmed visits found from 2020 to 2026 (Wikiloc tracks, TripAdvisor reviews, blogs, videos, press), about 1 to 3% of foreign visitors.
  2. Tourist arrivals: 69,831 air arrivals in 2023/2024, 68% from Europe, 19% from Africa and 13% from other regions; over 80,000 in 2024. We assume 1 to 4% of leisure tourists climb Mount Karthala.
  3. Field experience: according to Discovermores, the high season is very busy, mostly with young diaspora holidaymakers and schoolchildren. These groups post little on the open web: their share (about two thirds) is the most uncertain.
  4. Visitor profile: an assumed breakdown (sex, age, nationality, month, with whom) for each of the four groups, weighted by their size. These are assumptions, not measurements.
  5. Not counted: guides, drivers, scientific and technical staff, herders going up to their camps.

Sources : Comores Infos, 30/11/2024 ; Focus Océan Indien, 02/2026 ; Wikiloc, Grande Comore ; TripAdvisor (Mount Karthala) ; Fondation Sadev, Karthala crossing test mission (2024).

Five areas

What we measure

Visitor numbers

Number of trips and participants, tours chosen, busy periods, countries of residence.

Safety

Go, Adapt and No Go decisions and their reasons; incidents and follow-up.

Environment

Litter brought down per batch, state of the trails, wildlife and plant sightings.

Carbon

Kilometres driven and fuel used per trip, to recalculate the real footprint.

Local benefits

Guides, drivers and porters employed on each trip.

From the field to the dashboard

Where the data comes from

  1. The booking

    Tour, date, group size.

  2. Registration

    Before departure, each participant scans the team’s QR code and fills in a short form, for themselves and their group.

  3. The trip record

    The guide notes the safety decision, times, litter and sightings in an app that works offline.

  4. Feedback

    After the trip, participants give their opinion and report what they saw.

Our rules

  • No personal data is published: only anonymous totals.
  • Health information is read only by the supervising team.
  • The location of sensitive species is never made public.
  • Nature sightings are marked as validated or not by an expert.

The public counter says how many people climb Karthala. Data Pro says when, how, where they come from, in what conditions, and what the mountain keeps of it. For institutions, researchers and tourism professionals.
